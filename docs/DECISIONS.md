# Decision table

Every analytical choice, in the order it was made, with its reason. Where a later decision amends an earlier one, both are kept and the change is marked. Dates are 2026.

## Planning and data (9–11 November)

| ID | Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | 9 Nov | **Dataset:** Ng et al. 2019, ciprofloxacin recovery with two diet arms. | Controlled perturbation, longitudinal sampling, public raw reads and sample sheet. *Later finding: the diet arms' baselines were sequenced in different runs (D28, D29).* |
| D2 | 9 Nov | **Framing:** a prespecified reanalysis and robustness study, not discovery, and not blinded. | The published result is already known. |
| D3 | 9 Nov | **Humanised experiment first** (development and primary analysis); conventional experiment only as a later, conditional consistency check. | The humanised sample sheets show no ID conflicts. In the conventional sheets, 11 of 18 cecal sample IDs reappear on later faecal samples. |
| D4 | 9 Nov | **Conventional within-mouse analysis on hold** unless a prespecified identity check supports pairing. | ID reuse cannot be resolved from the sheet. |
| D5 | 9 Nov | **Baseline:** a single day-0 faecal sample in both arms; a multi-day baseline only as sensitivity. | Humanised STD mice have no samples before day 0; this gives the same baseline definition in both arms. |
| D6 | 9 Nov | **Recovery window: days 8–12;** day 13 only as sensitivity. | The repeat dose starts on day 14 in the humanised experiment, and the collection order around it is unconfirmed. |
| D7 | 9 Nov | **The cage is the independent unit;** per-cage estimates are reported first. | 2–3 cages per diet arm; mice in a cage share microbes. |
| D8 | 9 Nov | **No run-matched baseline check.** | The candidate samples are cecal contents, not faecal baselines. No valid run-matched baseline exists. |
| D9 | 9 Nov | **Each run is demultiplexed once,** and both workflows start from identical per-sample FASTQs with checksums. | Barcodes are reused across runs; this separates workflow differences from input differences. |
| D10 | 9 Nov | **No author contact;** each open question has a fixed default. | The reanalysis does not depend on unpublished information. |
| D11 | 11 Nov | **Repository content:** working notes stay outside the repository; the README and reference documents (including this table) are published. | A clean public record. |
| D12 | 11 Nov | **Nextflow only through nf-core/ampliseq (Stage 2).** Stage 1 and downstream analyses run as numbered scripts. | One machine and a linear pipeline. Running each QIIME 2 step directly also serves the project's learning aim. |
| D13 | 11 Nov | **Layout:** `metadata/`, `scripts/`, `config/` and `docs/` are tracked; `data/`, `results/` and `work/` are not. The depositors' `mapping_files.xlsx` is not committed. | Large data are rebuilt from code; the data licence is CC BY-NC. |
| D14 | 11 Nov | **Read layout,** from a check of read samples: 6 separate MiSeq runs; humanised reads 2 × 301 bp (conventional 2 × 251 bp); 12 bp index read; no primers in the reads (no primer trimming); 253 bp amplicon, so humanised reads are truncated before they run into adapter. *Barcode orientation was revised in D17.* | Read check against the sample sheet. |
| D15 | 11 Nov | **Run assignment:** sample-sheet run N = deposited file prep N for all six runs. In conventional run 2, 91 of 364 listed faecal samples have no reads in the deposited file; this is recorded as a data limitation. | The barcode census supports the mapping. The missing samples are conventional STD days 6–9, 12 and 13, and PD days −8 to −1; the humanised experiment is unaffected. |
| D16 | 11 Nov | **QIIME 2 2026.10 in its own conda environment,** built from the official environment file (in `config/`). | Its pinned dependencies cannot share an environment. Stage 2 uses Docker, so both approaches are shown. |

## Processing (12–13 November)

| ID | Date | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D17 | 12 Nov | **Demultiplexing:** `qiime demux emp-paired` per run, with Golay error correction and both reverse-complement options on. | The sheet's barcodes pass the Golay check only as reverse complements. In a simulation with the QIIME 2 decoder on the run-2 barcodes: 0–1 base errors gave 100% correct samples; 2 errors gave 88.6% correct, 11.4% unassigned and 0% wrong. |
| D18 | 12 Nov | **Sample IDs:** `H` + workbook ID (e.g. H2741); mice `H_m<n>`, cages `H_c<n>`. Repeated mouse-day entries are flagged, not merged. | No collision with conventional IDs; no IDs that look numeric to R or QIIME 2. 453 samples, 20 mice, 5 cages. |
| D19 | 12 Nov | **Two failed libraries excluded before denoising:** H3106 (38 read pairs) and H2956 (315). | A clear gap: every other sample has at least 8,930 read pairs. Both are outside the primary window. |
| D20 | 12 Nov | **DADA2 truncation rule,** set before denoising: the last position with median quality ≥ Q30 in every run. This gave 218/166 as primary and 200/150 as sensitivity. Each run denoised separately with identical settings: maxEE 2/2, truncQ 2, minimum overlap 12, independent inference, consensus chimera removal. *Primary setting changed in D20a.* | Quality profiles of all three runs; the weakest run decides. |
| D20a | 12 Nov | **Amendment, made after denoising and before any taxonomy, diversity or diet result was seen:** 200/150 becomes primary and 218/166 sensitivity. | Technical evidence only. Retention across runs: 0.708 / 0.705 / 0.732 at 200/150, against 0.692 / 0.623 / 0.712 at 218/166. Shared ASVs carry > 99.9% of reads in both. The 97 bp overlap still spans the full 253 bp amplicon. |
| D21 | 12 Nov | **Sensitivity rule** for biological results, fixed before any was seen. Richness and Faith's PD count as robust only if direction and approximate size agree between settings. The primary result is recomputed on the same mice, days and cages, and any change is reported. | Setting-specific ASVs are rare but numerous, so richness-type measures depend on processing. |
| D22 | 12 Nov | **Two technical comparisons of the settings:** per-sample retention by step, and same-sample Bray–Curtis between settings. | They show where the run-2 gain arises (broad filtering), and that profiles barely change (median 0.024). |
| D23 | 12 Nov | **H3058 and H3072,** two libraries with the same mouse-day label (H_m19, day 5): both kept in the table, both excluded from trajectories. | Bray–Curtis 0.504 between them, against 0.024 typical for one sample under two settings. Day 5 is outside the analysis window. |
| D24 | 12–13 Nov | **Taxonomy:** the official SILVA 144 V4 515F/806R classifier. Its size, MD5 and DOI were verified, and its GPG signature against the SILVA key. The same artifact and settings are used for both DADA2 settings and for the ampliseq ASVs. | The primers in its README match the EMP 515F/806R primers. Details in [ENVIRONMENT.md](ENVIRONMENT.md). |
| D25 | 13 Nov | **Taxon names as SILVA 144 gives them,** with an alias table to the paper's names (e.g. S24-7 = Muribaculaceae). Placeholder labels are not counted as resolved. Comparisons with the paper are made at family level. | SILVA 144 adds a kingdom rank and new phylum names. |
| D26 | 13 Nov | **Terasakiellaceae ASVs are interpreted only at order level** (Rhodospirillales). Cyanobacteriota ASVs are gut Ca. Gastranaerophilales. | Terasakiella is a marine genus, so the family label is implausible for mouse gut. A high classifier confidence measures agreement with the reference, not biological fit. |
| D27 | 13 Nov | **Depth:** 6,400 reads per sample, with repeated rarefaction (q2-boots, n = 100, without replacement, seed 20261113); alpha diversity averaged by mean, distance matrices by medoid. Rarefaction curves were checked first, grouped by run only. Unrarefied Bray–Curtis and Aitchison distance are sensitivity analyses. | All metrics are at plateau by 6,400 reads, and all 451 samples are retained. |
